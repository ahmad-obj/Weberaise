## 4) INSPIRATION SITES + WHAT WE ACTUALLY LEARNED FROM THEM

These were reviewed in depth and define the direction. Do not reduce them to “dark websites.”

### Nothin’
https://www.noth.in/
Most important reference for the hero.
Key lesson: a rigid/stable typographic composition can coexist with an unstable shader/material layer. Their visual field changes what parts/material states of typography are visible rather than simply placing a blob behind text. Hero spectacle then gives way to calmer editorial sections. Strong giant typography, scale contrast, weird-but-controlled material language, negative space, asymmetrical work presentation.
For WebeRaise: use the logic, not the exact objects/look.

### Andrew Reff
https://andrewreff.com/
Key lessons: enormous typography, reactive dot/grid field, GSAP/ScrollTrigger/SplitText-type choreography, perspective/depth transitions between otherwise flat planes, sticky/cinematic project presentation. Use interaction as an environment, not decoration.
Do not copy the personal AI-assistant angle unless we later build a genuinely useful website-audit assistant.

### OrfeoAI
https://www.orfeoai.com/
Useful for dark luxury-tech staging, restrained cyan/blue against near-black, layered depth planes, atmospheric lines/fields, minimal loading state, and business seriousness. Do not depend on its heavy 3D centerpiece. We want the layering/material restraint, not the 3D object.

### Neria Katz
https://www.neriakatz.com/
Useful for cinematic loading/state transitions and the feeling that the site is a designed sequence of chapters. We still want normal familiar scrolling, but major sections can behave like intentional “states.” Avoid overly playful loading jokes.

### Jerimy Brown
https://www.jerimybrown.com/index.html
Useful for reactive particle/field interaction and the idea that interactivity can be obvious briefly, then settle into subtle background behavior. One signature behavioral motif is better than ten disconnected microinteractions.

### Meinhard Taxer
https://meinhardtaxer.com/
Important for negative space, asymmetry, sparse black layouts, visual restraint and one memorable interaction. Do not panic-fill empty space. Three strong objects in a huge field can feel more premium than twelve cards.

### Obscura Studio
https://obscurastudio.webflow.io/
Important for agency voice, editorial chapter numbering, service framing as a method/system rather than a generic menu, restrained dark composition, and strategic problem framing. Imagery acts as punctuation rather than filling every section.

### Evolv-Ai / 21st.dev
https://21st.dev/@nextjsshop/templates/evolv-ai
Useful mainly for commercial/conversion anatomy and conventional landing-page clarity, not as the visual identity. We need the site to impress designers while still being obvious to a clinic/store/business owner.

The common pattern behind my taste:
**the layout itself is highly controlled while one layer is allowed to behave unpredictably.**
That is more important than “dark + animation.”

## 5) LOCKED HERO DIRECTION

We chose **Concept A (Fluid Identity) with pieces of Concept C (Controlled Collapse / recession).**

Hero should be a signature first viewport, not “heading + blue blob + buttons.”

### Hero visual concept

- Near-black `#070A0F` canvas.
- Minimal navigation/peripheral metadata.
- Huge `WEBERAISE` typography acts as part of the artwork, almost edge-to-edge.
- Base word state: sharp near-white.
- A large irregular fluid/shader field passes through/intersects the word.
- **Inside the fluid region the SAME typography changes material state**: restrained blue-white luminance, refraction/displacement, subtle glass/specular feeling, possibly very subtle edge chromatic separation.
- Outside fluid: typography remains clean and static.
- The fluid should often be perceived through its effect on content, not as an obvious big blob.
- Cursor influences the fluid boundary with inertia. Do not glue a blob directly to the mouse.
- Initial interaction can be noticeable; idle behavior becomes slow/subtle.
- Background may have very faint grain and a low-opacity point/grid field that becomes more visible only when disturbed.
- If WebGL fails, the DOM typography still needs to look excellent.

### Hero → Section 01 transition

On first scroll:
- huge typography moves slightly upward / scales down
- hero plane subtly recedes in perspective
- very restrained blur/luminance reduction
- shader stretches downward
- shader boundary becomes the reveal/mask for Section 01

It should feel like the first visual plane is being pulled/receded away, not “next div slides up.”

### Mobile

Do not reproduce desktop literally. Simplify. Potentially stack giant text as `WEBE / RAISE`, use a simpler vertical fluid mask, reduce pointer-dependent behavior, preserve the same visual concept.

### Intended technical approach later

Likely:
- Next.js / React
- GSAP + ScrollTrigger
- Lenis if it earns its cost
- WebGL/GLSL only for fluid mask/refraction/noise/pointer deformation
- DOM/CSS for everything that does not need WebGL
- reduced-motion and low-power fallbacks
